Push, Pull, Legs — Explained

The breakdown behind my 6-day split: what each day trains and why, plus a real tutorial for every single exercise. Push and pull are each merged from both my A/B variants below, so nothing's repeated twice.

🏋️

Push Day

What it trains: every muscle that presses weight away from your body — chest, front & side deltoids, and triceps. They all drive the same joint actions (shoulder flexion + elbow extension), so bench presses, overhead presses, and dips all recruit the same muscle group from different angles. Training them together in one session means you can go hard on all of them, then let the whole group recover together before the next push day.
🧲

Pull Day

What it trains: everything that pulls weight toward your body — lats, mid-back (rhomboids/traps), rear delts, and biceps. Deadlifts and rows build the back's thickness and width, face pulls and rear delt work balance out all the pressing from push day, and curls finish off the biceps — which are already pre-fatigued as a secondary mover on every row and pull-up anyway.
🦵

Legs Day

What it trains: the biggest muscles in the body — quads, hamstrings, glutes, and calves — through three movement patterns: squatting (knee-dominant), hinging (hip-dominant, e.g. Romanian deadlifts), and single-leg work (lunges). Core work gets tacked on at the end, since it's already been working overtime to stabilize every squat and hinge in the session.
